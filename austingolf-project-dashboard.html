Thirteen private courses cannot be booked; twelve semi-privates have conditional access. That is 58% of records where “Book Tee Time” is the wrong button — which is why the detail template needs two variants rather than one.

It is the direct expression of the access-scarce thesis and the reason a private club would ever pay for a listing.

Why the Hill Country tier changes the business

Municipal courses have no marketing budget. Semi-privates have small ones. Destination resorts buy travel marketing as a line item and are used to paying for placement — Horseshoe Bay, Boot Ranch, Escondido, Comanche Trace, Cordillera Ranch. That is the customer who can actually fund the site, and a golf-trip audience is precisely who they want to reach.

It also rescues the Golf Trail Passport, which is thin inside city limits and obvious as a Hill Country route. Visit Austin already markets Hill Country as part of the destination, so the scope is not a stretch.

Structure it as tiers, not one flat list: Austin Metro stays complete and authoritative; Hill Country is curated and destination-framed. Complete where completeness is the claim, selective where quality is.

Open decisions

4 need a call

1. The Jake Mercer byline

The homepage claims every course is reviewed, there is a byline page, and the GGC drafts carry the name. If Jake is not a real person, that is a specific liability in a market where twelve decision-makers control forty-three courses and all of them talk to each other. A GM asking “who is Jake?” and getting no good answer is worse than never having called.

Recommendation: publish under John Doumas. The whole sales frame is a named local handing GMs their customers' names. Your name on the reviews is a stronger asset than a house pseudonym, and it removes a landmine before it can go off.

2. Reviews — the visitor/sponsor conflict

You will be selling Verified and Featured listings to the same courses that user reviews would criticise. A negative top review on a paying course's page is a churn event and a reputational problem in a small market. An empty review section also reads as a dead site, and moderation is real work for one person.

Recommendation: build the data model now; launch with your own structured assessment plus the Conditions Report; add UGC reviews once there is traffic, gated to newsletter subscribers. From day one, run a private “Played it? Tell me what I got wrong” form — same input, none of the downside, and it builds relationships instead of friction.

3. Pricing — how much to publish

Exact green fees rot weekly and will make the site wrong in public. Rate structure is stable and is the thing visitors genuinely cannot find elsewhere.

Recommendation: publish bands, not numbers — weekend/weekday ranges, when twilight actually starts, resident vs non-resident, cart included or not, replay rates. Add a dated “what I paid” line, which ages honestly where an undated price does not. Launch fee data on the top 20 courses rather than all 60.

4. Hosting decision is stale

The locked decision log says SiteGround + WordPress + Kadence. Reality is Cloudflare Pages serving static HTML. Either update the decision or make the migration explicit — right now the plan and the deployment disagree.

Mapbox setup

replaces the Poncho hotspot section

Mapbox is the choice because Studio lets the basemap be built from the brand palette — a map that looks like AustinGolf rather than like Google. Free tier covers 50,000 loads/month, well beyond near-term traffic.

  1. Create the account and token Sign up at mapbox.com. Under Account → Tokens, create a token scoped to styles:read and fonts:read only, and add a URL restriction for austingolf.com. Public tokens are visible in the browser — the URL restriction is what protects it.
  2. Build the brand style in Studio Start from Mapbox Light, then override: land #F5F5F0, water #293E2F, parks and golf polygons #7A8471, roads in #D6D7CE with labels in #171A17. Turn off POI labels — they compete with the course pins. Golf course polygons exist in the base data, so greens read as green without any extra work.
  3. Structure the course data as GeoJSON One FeatureCollection, one feature per course, with id, name, type, holes, feeBand, and bookable in properties. Generate it from the existing COURSES array so there is a single source of truth rather than two lists to keep in sync.
  4. Colour pins by access type Terra #C4622D for bookable, olive #7A8471 for private. This makes the access-scarce thesis legible at a glance — a visitor sees instantly how much of Austin golf they cannot simply book.
  5. Cluster and filter Enable clustering at metro zoom. Wire the existing directory filters — type, area, budget, holes — to the map layer so filtering the list filters the map.
  6. Detail-page variant On a course page, reuse the same style zoomed to that course with drive-time context rather than a routing overlay. Hole-by-hole annotation is a later, per-course project — do not block launch on it.

Photography

hard constraint before Nov

What Koenig already has in the region

His Texas gallery covers Barton Creek, Spanish Oaks (Bee Cave), Boot Ranch (Fredericksburg) and Cordillera Ranch (Boerne). Three of those four are private — exactly the courses that cannot be photographed any other way — and the fourth is the tournament venue.

He is a represented professional: national magazine credits, 116K followers, booking 2026 shoots, collaboration requests routed through management. “Exposure on a pre-launch directory” is the pitch he declines weekly. Do not lead with it.

Three angles that are not that pitch:

  1. Broker the work rather than ask for free usage You are about to be talking to twelve decision-makers controlling forty-three properties. Bundling three or four Hill Country shoots into one Austin trip gives him paid work he would otherwise sell one at a time, gives courses assets they need, and lets your usage rights come in as a term of the deal you brokered. He already markets to destinations, not just individual courses.
  2. Use the tournament as the reason to call A PGA Tour event at Barton Creek in November is a legitimate hook for a golf photographer to be in Austin, and Omni is a brand worth his time.
  3. Print sales revenue share He already sells course prints as art. An Austin/Hill Country print shop on the site costs him nothing, earns him margin, and can carry usage rights as consideration.

Do not block launch on any of it

Sixty course pages need adequate, not exceptional. In order of effort: ask the courses for media-kit usage — free, and the ask doubles as a first sales touch; request Visit Austin media library access; pull resort press kits from Omni, Horseshoe Bay and Hyatt Lost Pines; shoot the munis yourself at dawn. An honest iPhone frame of Lions beats stock.
